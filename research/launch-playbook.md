# Launching Rain: what Paul Solt and kr0der actually say

## Read this first

- I read all 826 saved posts (98 from Paul Solt, 728 from kr0der) plus the posts they quote. I also read images, and the one long article, that carry the substance. Most of the file is about AI coding tools and model gossip. About 60 posts are about launching and selling apps.
- Neither person has launched a watch app. Paul launches Mac and iPhone utilities. kr0der is new to mobile (he says so) and sells weekly subscriptions on iPhone with paid ads. Nothing here is Apple Watch specific. The Watch facts come from Apple's own pages, and I say so where they do.
- The posts are thin on launch numbers. Paul gives one useful set (a feature spike on a Mac app). kr0der gives many numbers, nearly all showing that paid marketing did not work for him.
- Links are https://x.com/<handle>/status/<id>. I fetched some posts' images and quoted posts through a public mirror (fxtwitter) because the saved file holds only the text. No X API, no sign-in.
- "Backed by numbers" means the author posted their own figures. Everything else is opinion or a quoted third party.

## 2. The App Store page

### Screenshots (the best-supported topic in the material)

- Paul's article, written up from @DesignerAnts (Theodora), is the one detailed how-to ([link](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2037591726227902555), 925 likes, 562k views). Its rules:
  - Text carries the screenshot. Write the headline first, in 8 words or fewer.
  - Answer "is this for me?", not "what does it do?". Say what changes for the user.
  - Order them as a story: name the pain, state the shift, show proof, then one or two features.
  - Test: cover the UI and read only the headlines. Then run the new set for 30 days and check conversion in App Analytics.
- The numbers in it are not Paul's: "70% of screenshot effectiveness comes from the text overlay" and "an 80% conversion lift" for one app, both from the designer who sells the service. Treat them as a vendor claim. Paul also sells a PDF and links her paid service in the same article.
- kr0der's own data: product page views to download was about 50% (800 of 1,600) while his Meta ads got 1-2% click-through and almost no installs. He concluded the page was not the problem ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2077367076810055812)). One app, one niche.
- kr0der sells a screenshot generator (Screenies), so his screenshot advice has a commercial interest ([launch](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2089687761972527327)). He warns against AI copy that glues two phrases together ("Questions, answered") ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2097184155570487780)).
- Paul made the videos for his iPhone listing as 3D renders composited in DaVinci Resolve ([post](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2113497273690669285)).
- Apple's own spec, which I fetched from Apple (not from them): Apple Watch screenshots are required, 1 to 10 per size, and must be one of a fixed list. For Ultra 3 and Ultra 4 that is 422 x 514 px. The same size must be used across all localisations. The iPhone 6.3 inch size note that kr0der posted ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2118795059953017040)) is irrelevant to a watch-only app.

## 3. Pricing

This is where kr0der has the most numbers, and they come from weekly-subscription iPhone apps sold with paid ads, a different business from a €2 utility.

**kr0der, with his own numbers**
- 71 people reached his paywall and none converted ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2067503081794015591)). The first paid TikTok conversion, a $6.99 weekly plan, cost about $400 of ads ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2069642678502097037)).
- A user with five weekly renewals totalling $60 against a $50 annual plan: "I don't understand the psychology but it actually works" ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2076319677173973456)). The same product then had someone refund nine weekly payments at once ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2109075012292620631)).
- He offers a 50% off first week "checkout abandon" deal and counts several sales from it ([example](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2084069754286051804)). He later saw two weekly renewals that "started from the 50% off week 1" ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2086513346166325675)).
- His whole experiment ended at $5,087.81 after 105 days, most of it from X payouts rather than apps ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2110369060404015614)). His summary: selling is "10x harder than I thought", stop adding features and do marketing, "distribution is so hard", and he failed at TikTok ads, Meta ads, slideshows and cold outreach.

**kr0der, opinion and disagreement with himself**
- Offer weekly, monthly and annual for the anchoring effect, citing an Adapty article ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2071700288424304933)). Two weeks later he removed the monthly plan "to remove decision fatigue" ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2077287297750622514), quoting his earlier post). In August he ran weekly versus monthly as a test ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2084291733433819446)). He has not settled this.
- Never offer lifetime, and do not price annual very low ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2084405954309996818), quoting @LinoLeighton, who says annual should not go under $29.99). This is opinion. kr0der's guess at the reason (refund obligations if you pull the app) is a guess.
- Do not hardcode the paywall; use a paywall service (RevenueCat or Superwall) so prices can be tested without waiting for review ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2075803771649835395)). He moved to Superwall ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2077861838264672389)) and later to Adapty because he says RevenueCat's daily payouts are US only ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2081213165539610702)). I could not check that claim.
- Join the App Store Small Business Program for 15% instead of 30% ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2065901780953915783), credit to @athcanft; the image is Apple's welcome email).
- Localised prices vary a lot (his weekly plan is $9.99 in the US and $13.21 in Poland) and he queries whether that is worth it ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2078903451808153618)).

**Paul Solt.** No pricing advice saved. His listing shows the Mac app as one product with a plain rating-led page.

## 5. Launch day and after

Honest summary: neither describes a launch day. Both describe slow, mostly unsuccessful marketing.

**Paul Solt**
- His one big number: Apple featured Super Easy Timer on the Mac, and he reports 7,750 downloads and 370 new ratings since ([post](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2105675797277413588)). The chart he posted (26 weeks, units) shows a flat line near zero for about three months, then a spike of roughly 1,000 to 2,000 units a week, decaying over about six weeks to a plateau well above where it started, perhaps 400 a week. The weekly average over the period is 298. He does not say what week the feature began, and the chart implies it began in early July.
- Ratings grew from 59 at 4.7 stars ([screenshot, 7 Jul](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2074300962982773218)) to 499 at 4.9 stars on 21 October ([post](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2112909531491578283)). That span includes the featuring, so the growth is not from any one tactic.
- He plans to ship the iPhone and iPad version "next week", with no marketing plan stated.

**kr0der (own numbers, all marginal)**
- Meta ads: $20 AUD a day, no profitable ad. He killed ads at about $70 spend with no conversions ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2083206808282476613)). One good ad had spent $10 and got a $49.99 annual sub.
- TikTok slideshows: 500 to 1,000 views each, conversion unknown ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2078585354937143669)). He posted from several warmed-up accounts.
- UGC creators: "WAYYYY harder than I thought" to make look real ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2078340235323453881)).
- Apple Search Ads: he followed a guide and said Apple gives free credits ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2071533640404910405)). No result appears in the saved posts.
- Lesson he stated: stop overthinking posting ("who cares if you get shadowbanned") ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2077563365963272464)) and "spotlight syndrome" means nobody is judging you ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2081548878562931165)). These are about nerves, not tactics.
- Study successful apps' onboarding and paywalls on screensdesign (not sponsored, he says) ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2077407603806925165)).
- One sale came from a ChatGPT referral to his product site ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2105311988121588096)). Tiny sample.

**Where they disagree.** Paul's best result came from Apple featuring a plain, good utility. kr0der's lesson is that paid acquisition and content did not work for him. They do not argue with each other, but together they point away from paid marketing for a small utility.

## 7. Asking for reviews

**Paul Solt (opinion, with one anecdote).** He tried a personalised in-app prompt: his photo, a short note ("I built Super Easy Timer and I use it every day..."), his signature, and two buttons, "Maybe Later" and "Rate Super Easy Timer" ([post](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2073776933280625044)). A user wrote to say it "compelled me to take 2 minutes to leave you a review which I ordinarily would not've". That is one message, not a conversion figure.

**The conflict.** Apple's App Review Guideline 5.6.1 says "Use the provided API to prompt users to review your app ... we will disallow custom review prompts." (I read it on Apple's page.) Paul's prompt is a custom prompt on the Mac, and Apple approved that app in six minutes ([post](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2074300962982773218)). One approval does not make it permitted. Do not copy it into a watch app on the strength of Paul's post.

**kr0der (anecdote).** Another developer (@mobuildapps, quoted) said Apple banned review prompts in onboarding and he moved his prompt to after the user tried a feature, and the reviewer did not flag it ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2075387529076093176)). That is the system prompt placed later, which is the allowed route.

**Both, as tips.**
- Turn on App Store Connect mobile notifications for new reviews, and reply fast ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2078057623518433520), quoting @andilosing).
- A one-star review for a paywalled app that said "subscription required" in the description: he asks whether reporting works ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2092673689389351125)). No answer in the saved posts.

## 8. Getting through App Review

- Speed. Paul had a Mac update approved in 6 minutes ([post](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2074300962982773218)). kr0der saw under a day for every review, and one big update went from "In Review" to "Approved" in about 30 seconds ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2074467973805531146)). He guesses Apple uses AI to approve safe changes. Two weeks later he saw an app stay in review four days after he swapped payment infrastructure, while three small updates passed ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2081881608886624426)). He guesses review priority tracks the riskiness of the change. Both are guesses from his own submissions.
- Give reviewers a demo video. His prompt: "record a demo going through the app to make it easy for apple reviewers to review the app and then upload it via asc CLI" ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2117664246796538065)). The image is an Apple approval email dated 29 Oct, submitted "by API Key", which shows the flow works but not that the video made the difference.
- Automate App Store Connect with the `asc` CLI by Rudrank so the agent can do uploads, metadata and submissions ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2078856890575204704), tool at github.com/rorkai/App-Store-Connect-CLI). He says Codex configured a whole app including screenshots and he only tested and submitted ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2078288401812562066)).
- Do not trust an agent's judgement on marketing. His AI told him to pause an ad that had spent $2 and ignore bad ones ([post](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2083297399473397765)).
- From the Apple pages I read: Guideline 5.2.1 and 5.2.2 apply to data and names. Rain's data source and its use of any brand name in metadata fall under them. Neither person discusses this.

## 9. What I would do for Rain (my recommendation, not theirs)

This is my reading of the material for one watch-only utility by one person in the Netherlands. None of it has been tested.

1. **Price.** Do not copy kr0der's weekly-subscription model. His evidence is for iPhone apps with a paywall and ad budget, and it did not make money for him. A rain forecast is used daily, is easy to compare with free alternatives, and has a running cost only if you proxy the data. I would charge once, in the €2 to €4 range, with no subscription, unless the data source forces a server cost, in which case a single low annual price. I would enrol in the Small Business Program first. This is judgement: no source here covers one-off pricing for a watch utility.
2. **Name and metadata.** "Rain" is plain and descriptive, as Paul's names are. Do not put "Buienradar" in the name, subtitle, keywords or screenshots unless the owner permits it, because of Guideline 5.2.1/5.2.2. Subtitle should say the job, for example "Rain in the next 2 hours" (the exact words need your copy rules). Localise the page in English and Dutch at launch.
3. **Screenshots.** Follow Paul's article, because it is the only concrete practice in the material and it matches what I would do anyway. Five or fewer Ultra 3 shots (422 x 514), each with one headline of 8 words or fewer about the user's moment, not features: "Will I get wet?" first, then the circular complication on a real face ("In 30 min" at a glance), the Smart Stack card, the forecast, the radar. Use the renders in `renders/watch/` as the UI proof. Check conversion in App Analytics after 30 days. I would not buy a screenshot service.
4. **Preview video.** Apple's spec page lists none for Watch. Skip it. Put the moving radar in a short clip for your own site and social posts instead.
5. **Review prompts.** Use the system prompt only (`requestReview`), after the person has had a useful forecast several times, never from a button. Add a permanent "Rate Rain" link to the product page's write-review URL. Do not build Paul's personalised prompt. Remember it does nothing in TestFlight.
6. **App Review.** The reviewer cannot make it rain. Submit a demo video of the forecast, the complication states and the radar, plus a note saying where location is used and what the data source is. I would expect a demo mode or saved forecast build to help; this is my inference, not something either person said. Use the `asc` CLI if you want the agent to drive App Store Connect. Submit a simple first version early to clear the slow first review, as kr0der may suggest (see the unverified note in section 4).
7. **TestFlight.** Wear it for two weeks on the real Ultra 3, including a real commute in rain, and do two or three calls with Dutch cyclists watching them use it (Paul's and raroque's advice). Your own `reloads.log` plan already covers complication freshness.
8. **Launch.** Keep it cheap. One honest post and a Dutch page are within what these two actually did. I would skip paid ads and posting farms: kr0der's own conclusion is that they did not work for him, and Rain's market is small and local. Local channels (Dutch cycling and watch communities, Tweakers, Reddit r/thenetherlands) are my guess; neither person tested anything similar.
9. **Featuring.** Paul's featuring is the only big upside in the material. I would nominate Rain through App Store Connect once the page and a stable build are ready, and make sure it uses the newest watchOS 27 features Apple promotes: complications, Smart Stack, Digital Crown. I have no evidence on what wins a feature.
10. **Support.** Turn on new-review notifications and reply to every written review in the first month.
