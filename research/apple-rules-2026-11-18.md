# Apple rules for RainWatch problem screens, About page and copy

Sources read on 2026-11-18, from Apple's own pages (raw doc JSON and live pages). "Quote" means verbatim. "Inference" means mine, not Apple's. Where Apple is silent, it says so.

## 1. Allow Once: status during and after, re-asking, watchOS

**Plain answer: Yes. During Allow Once the status is `.authorizedWhenInUse`. When the app stops being "in use" it reverts to `.notDetermined`, and Apple says you may call `requestWhenInUseAuthorization()` whenever the status is `.notDetermined`, so the system asks again. Apple's own pages do not state a watchOS-specific difference. Apple Support says Allow Once works on watchOS 6 and later.**

- `requestWhenInUseAuthorization()` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/requestwheninuseauthorization()), watchOS 2.0+:
  - "You may call requestWhenInUseAuthorization() whenever the current authorization status is not determined (CLAuthorizationStatus.notDetermined)."
  - Option table: "Allow While Using App | When In Use authorization that does not expire." / "Allow Once | Temporary When In Use authorization that expires when the app is no longer in use." / "Don't Allow | Denied; no further authorization requests are allowed."
  - "If the user's choice grants temporary When In Use authorization, the authorization expires when the app is no longer in use, reverting to Not Determined status (CLAuthorizationStatus.notDetermined)."
  - "Important: Your app must be in the foreground to show a location authorization prompt."
  - "If the initial authorization status is anything other than CLAuthorizationStatus.notDetermined, this method does nothing and doesn't call the locationManager(_:didChangeAuthorization:) method."
- `requestAlwaysAuthorization()` page (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/requestalwaysauthorization()): "Allow Once | Core Location grants your app a Temporary When in Use authorization. The delegate receives CLAuthorizationStatus.authorizedWhenInUse. This authorization expires when your app is no longer in use, reverting to CLAuthorizationStatus.notDetermined."
- `locationManagerDidChangeAuthorization(_:)` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanagerdelegate/locationmanagerdidchangeauthorization(_:)): "When an app has temporary authorization, the authorization changes when the user ceases to use the app." Also: "If the user's choice doesn't change the authorization status after you call the requestWhenInUseAuthorization() or requestAlwaysAuthorization() method, the location manager doesn't report the current authorization status to this method—the location manager only reports changes."
- "In use" definition, "Choosing the Location Services Authorization to Request" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/choosing_the_authorization_level_for_location_services): "On all platforms that support When In Use authorization, an app is considered in use: When the app runs in the foreground. In the few seconds after an app leaves the foreground, a short grace period for your app to finish any current location tasks the user initiated. When the app shows the background location usage indicator... on watchOS it's a small icon." Also: "On watchOS there's no way to opt out of showing the background location usage indicator."
- Apple Support, "About privacy and Location Services in iOS, iPadOS, and watchOS" (https://support.apple.com/102515): "With iOS 13 or later, iPadOS 13 or later, and watchOS 6 or later, you can tap Allow Once to let an app access Location Services data only for one session (One Time). If you close and then re-open the app and it tries to access your location again, it will ask for your permission again."
- WWDC20 "What's new in location" (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10660): "if a user selects 'Allow Once' on your companion watch app or does a temporary accuracy upgrade, your phone app will be able to benefit from that decision." (Allow Once status is shared between watch and iPhone.)
- CLServiceSession (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clservicesession-pt7n), watchOS 11+: "The CLServiceSession object requests a person's authorization to meet those requirements if possible, including automatically re-asking as needed after temporary authorization lapses due to time your app spends in the background."
- Generic page "Requesting authorization to use location services" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/requesting-authorization-to-use-location-services): "After the initial interruption, the system stores your app's authorization status and doesn't prompt again." This is true for Allow While Using App and Don't Allow. It is not a contradiction for Allow Once (the status goes back to not determined), but the page does not mention Allow Once.

Gaps:
- No Apple page says "Allow Once on watchOS reverts to notDetermined after N minutes". The only wording is "when the app is no longer in use" (foreground, few seconds of grace, or background indicator).
- Apple's docs do not say whether a watchOS app that is raised again within a short time keeps the Allow Once. Inference: it counts as the same session only while "in use" continues.
- Apple's docs never say the authorizationStatus is `.notDetermined` on watchOS *at the moment of relaunch*. They say it reverts. Inference: on relaunch you will see `.notDetermined` and can ask again.

## 2. Don't Allow on watchOS: open Settings from the app, and the path to change it

**Plain answer: No. There is no Apple API to open Settings or the app's privacy page on watchOS. `openSettingsURLString` is not available on watchOS and `openSystemURL` accepts only `tel:` and `sms:`. The user has to go to Settings themselves. Apple documents the path as Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services (on the watch, or on the paired iPhone).**

- `UIApplication.openSettingsURLString` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiapplication/opensettingsurlstring): platforms listed "iOS 8.0+ iPadOS 8.0+ Mac Catalyst 13.1+ tvOS visionOS 1.0+". watchOS is not listed.
- `WKApplication.openSystemURL(_:)` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkapplication/opensystemurl(_:)), watchOS 7.0+: "url: A URL that supports the tel: or sms: scheme." "Use this method to initiate phone calls or send messages." The older `WKExtension.openSystemURL(_:)` page (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/WatchKit/WKExtension/openSystemURL(_:)) says the same.
- SwiftUI `openURL` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/openurl), watchOS 7.0+: "The system provides a default open URL action with behavior that depends on the contents of the URL. For example, the default action opens a Universal Link in the associated app if possible, or in the user's default web browser if not." No settings scheme is documented. There is no documented Settings URL for watchOS in Apple's docs.
- Apple Developer Forums thread (https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/694269), a developer asks exactly this: "I know that in iOS/macOS you can use the openSettingsURLString string from UIApplication to do what I want, but it's not available in watchOS and I haven't found any equivalent resource inside the WKExtension class". The thread shows no Apple answer. A 2026 reply asks for Apple to look at it.
- HIG "Settings" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings), watchOS: "In watchOS, apps and games don't add custom settings to the system-provided Settings app." The "provide a button that opens it directly" advice in that page is for the other platforms.
- HIG "Writing" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing): "If you need to direct someone to a setting, provide a direct link or button, rather than trying to describe its location." On watchOS no such link exists, so inference: you must describe the path in text.
- Path, Apple Personal Safety User Guide (https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/manage-location-services-settings-ips9bf20ad2f/web): "On your Apple Watch: Go to Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services." That page's per-app steps are written for iPhone and iPad.
- Per-app path on iPhone, Apple Support (https://support.apple.com/en-us/102647): "Open the Settings app and tap Privacy & Security, then select Location Services... Scroll down to find the app. Tap the app and select an option: Never... Ask Next Time Or When I Share... While Using the App... Always". On watchOS the options shown are not listed by Apple.
- Apple Watch User Guide, "View app settings and storage on Apple Watch" (https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/view-app-settings-and-storage-apd31a7d203c/watchos): "If you change them on your iPhone, the same changes are applied to your Apple Watch. To adjust these settings, use your iPhone. Go to the Settings app on your iPhone. Change any of the following settings: Privacy & Security: Turn on or off features such as Location Services, Analytics, Contacts, and Health."
- `CLAuthorizationStatus.denied` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clauthorizationstatus): "The user denied the use of location services for the app or they are disabled globally in Settings." `restricted`: "The app is not authorized to use location services."
- `locationManagerDidChangeAuthorization` page lists how status changes: "Change an app's location authorization in Settings > Privacy > Location Services, or in Settings > (the app) > Location Services. Turn location services on or off globally in Settings > Privacy > Location Services. Choose Reset Location & Privacy in Settings > General > Reset."

Gaps:
- Apple does not document the exact per-app screen on the watch itself (whether the app has a row under Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services on watchOS, or only on the paired iPhone). The watch-user-guide page says to use the iPhone for these settings. Inference: the safe copy points at the iPhone, "Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services", then the app. I did not test it on a watch.
- I found no watchOS 26 or 27 addition that opens Settings. I did not find a watchOS 27 release note that says so either way. Absence in the docs I read is not proof.
- The `WKApplication.openSystemURL` page is dated watchOS 7.0 and I could not see whether the watchOS 27 version adds schemes. The JSON on 2026-11-18 still lists only `tel:` and `sms:`.
- Inference: a Don't Allow cannot be undone by re-asking. The `requestWhenInUseAuthorization()` page says "Denied; no further authorization requests are allowed" and "this method does nothing" when the status is not `.notDetermined`.

## 3. CLServiceSession / CLLocationUpdate diagnostics after denial

**Plain answer: They tell you *why* the goal is not met, but give no way to re-prompt after denial and no system explanation screen. `authorizationDenied` means the user said no. Apple recommends handling it in your own UI by offering an alternative (for example picking a location). No Apple text says what to show.**

- `CLServiceSession` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clservicesession-pt7n), watchOS 11.0+: "An object that provides diagnostics about an app's authorization to use location services." "Each object provides diagnostics that your app can observe to understand how its authorization state may differ from the goal it expressed."
- `CLServiceSession.Diagnostic` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clservicesession-pt7n/diagnostic), watchOS 11.0+, properties: `alwaysAuthorizationDenied`, `authorizationDenied`, `authorizationDeniedGlobally`, `authorizationRequestInProgress`, `authorizationRestricted`, `fullAccuracyDenied`, `insufficientlyInUse`, `serviceSessionRequired`. The per-property pages (for example https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationupdate/authorizationdenied) carry only a signature and no description. Apple has no written definition.
- `CLLocationUpdate` has the same flags: `accuracyLimited`, `authorizationDenied`, `authorizationDeniedGlobally`, `authorizationRequestInProgress`, `authorizationRestricted`, `insufficientlyInUse`, `locationUnavailable`, `serviceSessionRequired`, `stationary` (watchOS 11.0+ per the doc JSON).
- WWDC24 "What's new in location authorization" (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10212/), transcript lines:
  - "Diagnostic Properties tell you why, when an authorization goal cannot be met."
  - "[.authorizationDenied is] that the person using your app does not want to share their location with it."
  - ".insufficientlyInUse for when Core Location can't yet ask for authorization on your app's behalf"
  - ".authorizationDeniedGlobally, which will be true if location services are disabled system-wide... your app will also be subject to that denial, so .authorizationDenied will be true as well."
  - Sample comment in Apple's code: "if (diagnostic.authorizationDenied) { // Ok, let's let them pick a location instead?"
  - "Core Location won't ask people to adjust your app's authorization level while it's in the background."
  - "If you need to know when they have made up their mind... `if !diagnostic.authorizationRequestInProgress`".
- "Suspending authorization requests" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/suspending-authorization-requests): you can defer the system prompt until your own pre-screen: "Obtain a session. This causes Core Location to display the authorization prompt." This needs the Info.plist key `CLRequireExplicitServiceSession`.
- "Configuring your app to use location services" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/configuring-your-app-to-use-location-services): "If location data is unavailable for any reason, make the best app experience you can without it. Disable features that rely on location data or provide alternatives to get the behavior you need." Also "Disabling features in advance is a more reliable approach than using a service and responding to errors."
- HIG Alerts (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts): "If your app detects a problem at startup, like no network connection, consider alternative ways to let people know. For example, you could show cached or placeholder data and a nonintrusive label that describes the problem." and "Avoid showing an alert when your app starts."

Gaps:
- No `CLServiceSession` API re-prompts after `.denied`. Apple's text for the `requestWhenInUseAuthorization()` method says no further requests are allowed after Don't Allow. No page shows a system "explanation" UI.
- Apple does not say what an app should show on denial, apart from "provide alternatives" and the WWDC sample comment "let them pick a location instead". The HIG Writing page covers the error-message style (section 6).
- The iOS docs show `CLServiceSession` re-asks "as needed after temporary authorization lapses". Inference: that is the only automatic re-ask, and only for lapsed Allow Once, not for Don't Allow.

## 4. App Review Guidelines: privacy policy link, permission, location

**Plain answer: The policy link must be in App Store Connect and "within the app in an easily accessible manner". Apple does not say first screen or before permission. 5.1.1(iv) and 5.1.5 push you to let people who refuse still use the app where possible and to explain the location purpose.**

Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ (read on 2026-11-18).

- 5.1.1(i): "Privacy Policies: All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner. The privacy policy must clearly and explicitly: Identify what data, if any, the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses of that data. Confirm that any third party with whom an app shares user data... will provide the same or equal protection of user data as stated in the app's privacy policy and required by these Guidelines. Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data."
- 5.1.1(ii): "Permission: Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection, even if such data is considered to be anonymous at the time of or immediately following collection. Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data. Apps must also provide the customer with an easily accessible and understandable way to withdraw consent. Ensure your purpose strings clearly and completely describe your use of the data."
- 5.1.1(iii): "Apps should only request access to data relevant to the core functionality of the app and should only collect and use data that is required to accomplish the relevant task."
- 5.1.1(iv): "Access: Apps must respect the user's permission settings and not attempt to manipulate, trick, or force people to consent to unnecessary data access... Where possible, provide alternative solutions for users who don't grant consent. For example, if a user declines to share Location, offer the ability to manually enter an address."
- 5.1.5: "Use Location Services in your app only when it is directly relevant to the features and services provided by the app... Ensure that you notify and obtain consent before collecting, transmitting, or using location data. If your app uses Location Services, be sure to explain the purpose in your app; refer to the Human Interface Guidelines for best practices for doing so."
- 5.2.5 (last sentence): "If your app displays Apple Weather data, it should follow the attribution requirements provided in the WeatherKit documentation."
- 2.3.3: "Screenshots should show the app in use, and not merely the title art, login page, or splash screen."

Gaps:
- No wording requires the link on the first screen, before the permission prompt, or on each screen. "Easily accessible" is undefined. Inference: a clearly labelled About row reachable from the main screen meets the text. Hiding it from first-launch and problem screens is not forbidden by the wording, but I cannot say how a reviewer reads "easily accessible" if the About page is not reachable when no location is granted. Inference: keep it reachable from the main view in the normal state, and the privacy policy also lives in App Store Connect.
- The guidelines do not say a watchOS app needs its own link, but "All apps" is the wording.
- 5.1.1(iv) alternative solutions: "Where possible". For RainWatch this alternative would be a manual place. The guideline does not make this mandatory. I did not check whether you plan one.

## 5. WeatherKit attribution

**Plain answer: Wherever you display Apple weather data you must clearly show the Apple Weather mark and the legal link. For apps that cannot open the link in a Safari view, `legalAttributionText` must be made available. Apple does not say "on every screen" or "on the same screen" in the pages I could read. RainWatch uses KNMI radar data and may not display Apple weather data on all screens, so the trigger is "displays any weather data from Apple".**

- "Get started with WeatherKit" (https://developer.apple.com/weatherkit/get-started/), section "Apple Weather and third-party attribution": "If your apps, web apps, or websites display any weather data from Apple (other than weather alerts or value-added services or products, as described below), you must clearly display the Apple Weather trademark ( Weather), as well as the legal link to other data sources."
- Value-added products: "When displaying value-added services or products, you agree to attribute the source of the data to "Weather", along with a notice that the data provided by Apple has been modified."
- Weather alerts: "All weather alerts displayed must contain an embedded link to the Apple weather alert details page provided to you by Apple. All weather alert titles or descriptions must contain the full name of the source meteorological agency that issued the alert."
- `WeatherAttribution` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weatherattribution): "Attribution is required for publishing software using WeatherKit." Properties: `combinedMarkLightURL`, `combinedMarkDarkURL` ("A URL for the combined 'Apple Weather' mark"), `squareMarkURL`, `legalPageURL` ("A link to the legal attribution page that contains copyright information about the weather data sources"), `serviceName`.
- `legalAttributionText` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weatherattribution/legalattributiontext), watchOS 9.4+: "This property returns text that should be made available to users for apps that cannot display the attributionURL contents in a Safari view. It contains language that outlines the weather data sources attribution and is a legal requirement of using WeatherKit."
- `WeatherService.attribution` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/weatherkit/weatherservice/attribution): "The required attribution which includes a legal attribution page and Apple Weather mark."
- WWDC22 "Meet WeatherKit" (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10003/): "Note that the Apple Weather mark and attribution link opens in an SFSafariViewController." "The first requirement is attribution. As you saw in my demo, you'll get a link from our Attribution API which you'll need to display in your native or web app. The second requirement is an attribution logo."
- Apple Developer Program License Agreement, Attachment 8 (https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/): "1.4 Your Application or Corresponding Product may access the Apple Weather Data only through the WeatherKit APIs. You agree that Your display of the Apple Weather Data must comply with all applicable attribution requirements and any other specifications provided in the Program Requirements. You may not modify, change, alter, or obscure Weather Alerts in any way." and "2.2 For Applications or Corresponding Products that use the WeatherKit APIs for real-time weather guidance, You must have an end user license agreement that includes the following notice: YOUR USE OF THIS REAL TIME WEATHER GUIDANCE APPLICATION OR WEBSITE IS AT YOUR SOLE RISK. WEATHER DATA MAY NOT BE ACCURATE."
- The ADPLA also says (3.3.1 G) "a watchOS App should have a user interface designed for quick interactions in accordance with the HIG's watchOS design themes".

Gaps:
- Apple's pages do not say which screen. "Clearly display" is the only placement word. A hidden About page that is reachable from the main screen is not ruled out in the wording, but "clearly" is not defined. Inference: put the mark and legal text where the Apple data is shown, or at least on a page one tap from there. I could not find a "where on screen" rule in Apple's docs or the ADPLA excerpt.
- Safari views do not exist on watchOS. Apple's docs name `legalAttributionText` as the answer for "apps that cannot display the attributionURL contents in a Safari view" and do not describe the watchOS UI (scrolling text, a link). Inference: a scrolling About page with the mark and `legalAttributionText` is the Apple-documented route on watch.
- ADPLA Attachment 8 sections 1.4 and 2.2 are the only parts I could read in the excerpt I got. Section 2.2 may apply to RainWatch if it counts as "real-time weather guidance". Check this with the user. I did not read the full agreement text.
- Attribution applies only if you display Apple Weather data. KNMI credit (CC BY 4.0) is not an Apple requirement. It comes from the licence, not from this research.

## 6. Apple UI copy guidance (HIG)

**Plain answer: Short, clear, verb-led button labels; title-style capitalisation for button titles ("Try Again"); sentence-style capitalisation for informative text and purpose strings; blank screens need a button or link for the next step; error messages avoid blame and say how to fix it; a pre-permission screen has one button ("Continue" or "Next", never "Allow"), no cancel and no close.**

Writing (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing):
- "Be clear. Choose words that are easily understood and convey the right thing. Check each word to be sure it needs to be there. If you can use fewer words, do so."
- "Be action oriented. Active voice and clear labels help people navigate through your app from one step to the next... When labeling buttons and links, it's almost always best to use a verb. Prioritize clarity and avoid the temptation to be too cute or clever with your labels. For example, just saying "Send" often works better than "Let's do it!""
- "Adopt capitalization rules that align with your app's style, then apply them consistently. While certain components, like buttons, have specific guidelines, how you format text reflects your app's voice. Title case is generally considered formal, while sentence case is more casual."
- "Avoid using we altogether because it may be unclear who the "we" in question refers to. This is particularly problematic in error messages like "We're having trouble loading this content." Something like "Unable to load content" is much clearer."
- "Write for how people use each device... iPhone and Apple Watch, for example, offer opportunities for personalization, but their small screens require brevity."
- "Provide clear next steps on any blank screens. An empty state... can provide a good opportunity to make people feel welcome and educate them about your app... An empty screen can be daunting if it isn't obvious what to do next, so guide people on actions they can take, and give them a button or link to do so if possible. Remember that empty states are usually temporary, so don't show crucial information that could then disappear."
- "Write clear error messages. It's always best to help people avoid errors. When an error message is necessary, display it as close to the problem as possible, avoid blame, and be clear about what someone can do to fix it. For example, "That password is too short" isn't as helpful as "Choose a password with at least 8 characters." Remember that errors can be frustrating. Interjections like "oops!" or "uh-oh" are typically unnecessary and can sound insincere."
- "Compare the tone of these two examples from Apple Watch. In the first, the tone is straightforward and direct, reflecting the seriousness of the situation." (page text; the examples are images).
- "Use possessive pronouns sparingly... "Favorites" conveys the same message as "Your Favorites," and is more succinct."
- "Give clear guidance and use consistent language throughout processes with multiple steps... Begin with language like "Get Started" to indicate you're starting a flow... use terms like "Continue" or "Next," but be consistent... Make it clear when a flow is complete by using language like "Done.""
- "Keep settings labels clear and simple." "If you need to direct someone to a setting, provide a direct link or button, rather than trying to describe its location."
- Page ends: "No additional considerations for iOS, iPadOS, macOS, tvOS, visionOS, or watchOS." So Apple has no separate watchOS writing rules.

Buttons (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons):
- "Consider using text when a short label communicates more clearly than an icon. To use text, write a few words that succinctly describe what the button does. Using title-style capitalization, consider starting the label with a verb to help convey the button's action — for example, a button that lets people add items to their shopping cart might use the label "Add to Cart.""
  (The link "title-style capitalization" goes to the Apple Style Guide: https://help.apple.com/applestyleguide/#/apsgb744e4a3?sub=apdca93e113f1d64.)
- watchOS section: "Prefer buttons that span the width of the screen for primary actions in your app. Full-width buttons look better and are easier for people to tap. If two buttons must share the same horizontal space, use the same height for both, and use images or short text titles for each button's content."
- "Append a trailing ellipsis to the title when a push button opens another window, view, or app."

Alerts (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts):
- "Create succinct, logical button titles. Aim for a one- or two-word title that describes the result of selecting the button. Prefer verbs and verb phrases... As with all button titles, use title-style capitalization and no ending punctuation."
- "If the title is a complete sentence, use sentence-style capitalization and appropriate ending punctuation. If the title is a sentence fragment, use title-style capitalization, and don't add ending punctuation."
- "Include informative text only if it adds value. If you need to add an informative message, keep it as short as possible, using complete sentences, sentence-style capitalization, and appropriate punctuation."
- "Avoid using an alert merely to provide information... prefer finding an alternative way to communicate it within the relevant context."
- "Avoid showing an alert when your app starts."
- "No additional considerations for tvOS or watchOS."

Privacy (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy):
- "Request permission only when your app clearly needs access to the data or resource... Avoid requesting permission at launch unless the data or resource is required for your app to function. People are less likely to be bothered by a launch-time request when it's obvious why you're making it. For example, people understand that a navigation app needs access to their location before they can benefit from it."
- Purpose string: "Aim for a brief, complete sentence that's straightforward, specific, and easy to understand. Use sentence case, avoid passive voice, and include a period at the end." Examples: "The app records during the night to detect snoring sounds." (good) / "Microphone access is needed for a better experience." (passive, vague) / "Turn on microphone access." (imperative, no reason).
- Pre-permission screen: "Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert... Use a term like "Continue" or "Next" to title the single button in your custom screen or window, clarifying that its action is to open the system alert."
- "Don't include additional actions in your custom screen or window, unless needed to obtain a legal consent. For example, don't provide a way for people to leave the screen or window without viewing the system alert — like offering an option to close or cancel."
- "Don't display a custom screen that mirrors the functionality of the system alert. In particular, don't create a button title that uses "Allow" or similar terms, because people don't allow anything in a pre-alert screen."
- "Never precede the system-provided alert with a custom screen or window that could confuse or mislead people."
- Location button: "In iOS, iPadOS, and watchOS, Core Location provides a button so people can grant your app temporary authorization to access their location at the moment a task needs it." "If your app has no authorization status, tapping the location button has the same effect as when a person chooses Allow Once in the standard alert. If people previously chose While Using the App, tapping the location button doesn't change your app's status." "If you know that people often grant your app Allow Once permission, consider using the location button to help them benefit from sharing their location without having to repeatedly interact with the alert." System titles named: "Current Location" or "Share My Current Location." (API: `LocationButton`, SwiftUI, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/CoreLocationUI/LocationButton, "A button that grants one-time location authorization"; I did not confirm the watchOS availability from the docs page, check it before use.)
- Launching (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching): "Launch instantly." and "Avoid creating a screen that looks like a splash screen or an "About" window" (the second line is about launch screens, which watchOS does not require).

Gaps:
- Apple states "Try Again" vs "Try again" rule only indirectly: alerts and buttons pages say title-style for button titles. So "Try Again" follows Apple. The Writing page lets you choose your own style for other elements but "certain components, like buttons, have specific guidelines".
- The Apple Style Guide page was not opened. Title-style rules for small words (a, of, to) come from there.
- No watchOS-specific copy rules exist in the HIG pages above ("No additional considerations... watchOS").
- Inference on "one button": the HIG "one button" rule is about custom screens *before* a system alert. A problem screen *after* denial is not covered by it. The Writing page ("give them a button or link to do so if possible") applies.

## 7. App Store product page text and captions

**Plain answer: Apple's product-page guidance says screenshots should be images from the app UI and each should "focus on a main benefit or feature". It gives no caption rules (the word "caption" does not appear on the page). Guideline 2.3.3 lets screenshots include "text and image overlays".**

- "Screenshots" section, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/: "Use images captured from your app's UI to visually communicate your app's user experience.
