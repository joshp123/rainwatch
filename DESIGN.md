# Design

How Rain looks and why. Read it before changing any view. The rules come from Apple's design principles (Human Interface Guidelines, "Design principles", June 2026), Emil Kowalski's `apple-design` skill (Apple's WWDC design talks), Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines, rams.ai, Ibelick's `baseline-ui` and `improve-ui` and Jakub Krehel's `better-ui`, all from Josh's bookmarks, and from Josh's own corrections.

## Rules

1. **Look like Apple's own, beside Apple's own.** Josh sees Rain next to Apple's complications, so it matches them. The circle sits on `AccessoryWidgetBackground`, the grey disc Sunrise and World Clock use. Its number is as tall as their main line and its unit as tall as their label, measured on his watch (`renders/watch/circular-states.png`): the number in white, the words around it in Buienradar blue, as World Clock colours "SF" orange. The lead word ("IN", "FOR") is smaller, because three lines at their size don't fit inside the arc. Complications use SF Compact Rounded; the app uses the system's text styles (`title3`, `body`, `footnote`), its margins (`scenePadding`), Swift Charts, toolbar buttons and `ContentUnavailableView`.
2. **One accent.** Buienradar blue (#5A9BD3) means rain and labels rain words. White is a value, grey is secondary. Red appears only for downpours on the radar.
3. **Never cover what matters.** On the radar the map round the yellow dot stays visible: the timeline sits on a solid black band at the bottom edge (map labels showing through read as part of it), and the map centres the dot in the part the band leaves free.
4. **Every element earns its place.** One label per fact: the radar names its moment once, relative to now ("in 25 min"), so it is never mistaken for the watch's clock. No decoration, no repeated words.
5. **Nothing touches, nothing shrinks.** Words never touch the line or the edge. Never box text shorter than its line height: watchOS then shrinks it, which made the circle look tiny.
6. **Words never contradict.** The circle's words are always about rain: "IN" until it arrives, "FOR" while it lasts. Wording that means opposite things in different states fails the glance.
7. **The line carries the meaning.** Height is how hard it rains, position is time. One scale everywhere (rain in mm/h, a little compressed, full height at Buienradar's Heavy line of 11.3 mm/h, so a burst of heavy rain stands clear of light rain; until November 2026 it was the square root of Buienradar's graph height, on which light and heavy rain looked alike in the circle), a smooth monotone curve, a bright edge over a fainter fill, nothing drawn in dry time, and a hole cut in the drawing round the change dot, so it survives tinted faces, which ignore dark rings and blend-mode cut-outs (round in the slot; straight across the radii in the circle, where a round hole leaves a fleck of a short spell). A layout that squeezes the line flat gives up words first (the rectangular slot drops its second line in short slots).
8. **Every state is designed.** Dry, drizzle, showers on and off, heavy, rain all window, stale, no location, offline, a failed refresh over a saved forecast (said at the top, with its age and Try Again), an hour-old KNMI forecast, no forecast from Apple and the radar unreachable each have a screen. With location refused and no place chosen, the face says so too ("No PLACE"), never the last position. `RainUITests` renders them all.
9. **Motion only where it explains.** The radar plays because the point is the time dimension; the crown pauses it and a tap or the play button resumes it. Its frames are KNMI's 1 km cells drawn smoothly, as Buienradar's picture is (`server/evidence/`): close up each wet cell keeps a soft but visible edge, so drizzle reads as rain rather than haze; wide out the cells are blended into each other; drizzle see-through in Buienradar blue, heavier rain brighter towards white, red from 10 mm/h, a grey veil where the radar has no data. With Reduce Motion on, the radar waits on the present until asked to play, and zooms without animation.
10. **Say no more than the data knows.** Age is the age of the data (KNMI's radar time, Apple's forecast time), never of the fetch. A KNMI run covers two hours from its radar time, so a forecast ten minutes old reaches 110 minutes ahead: the line, the circle's track and the words stop there ("Dry for over an hour", not "over 2 hours"), and past it the baseline is dotted. "No data" is never drawn or worded as dry. Apple's hourly forecast knows rain only by clock hour, so its words name hours ("Dry until 15:00", "IN <1 HR") and the page says "Hourly forecast", or "Hourly forecast made 23:40" once it is old.
11. **Name the source.** "Source: KNMI" with KNMI's data (its CC BY 4.0 licence); Apple's Weather mark and legal link with Apple's (its terms). On the watch the legal link opens the About page, which shows Apple's own legal text (`legalAttributionText`: the watch has no Safari view for Apple's page) and says that RainWatch changed Apple's data into rain or dry steps. The rectangular slot's and the Smart Stack's second line reads " Weather" (or " Weather, hourly") for Apple's data, in place of the hour labels where the slot is short, and keeps it once old (" Weather, made 23:40"); the circular, corner and inline slots have no room for it. The About page, reached from the end of the forecast page (Josh, 18 November 2026: not on the first-launch and problem screens), also has the privacy policy and KNMI's licence, its data's links and a note that RainWatch changed it.
12. **Places by their town's name.** The forecast for where you are is titled with the town, from Apple Maps' reverse lookup with the position rounded to about 1 km, beside Apple's location arrow ("My Location" until the name is known). A picked place keeps Apple Maps' name for it. Each search result shows its Apple map, the case Apple's Maps terms name (Developer Program License Agreement, Attachment 6, 2.4). Build 4 used "My Location" and the typed words instead, to stay clear of 2.4 and 2.5; Josh chose the town names on 18 November 2026, and if App Review objects, the fix is a resubmission with "My Location".
